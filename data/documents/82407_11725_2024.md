As CEO of Parlevliet & Van der Plas (“PP Group”), one of the world’s

leading fish and seafood companies, I am proud to present our 2022

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) report. Since our founding in

1950, our family-owned company has grown to over 10,000 dedicated

employees, striving to provide affordable and healthy food to millions of

## people worldwide every day.

Sustainability is at the core of our mission. Our CSR strategy focuses on

preserving the capital of the sea by ensuring responsible and sustainable

practices. This involves obtaining and maintaining certifications for all

fisheries our vessels operate in, working within responsible standards,

and balancing ecological, social, and economic interests as stewards of

## the ocean.

In 2022, in the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic, we prioritized rebuilding

relationships and strengthening bonds through face-to-face interactions

and emphasizing our people-oriented approach. We hosted expert group

sessions, engaged in open dialogue with our crew, and implemented

safety measures across our operations. Our three CSR pillars - Healthy

Oceans, Happy Planet, and Happy People - have guided us through these

## efforts.

This CSR report highlights our activities and the impact we made in 2022,

while also considering the broader context of past years. We have further

developed our sustainability policies, empowered our employees to

take ownership of various sustainability goals. As we move forward, we

will continue to implement these policies, measure our progress, and

evaluate our achievements in the short and long term.

Many interesting projects were conducted, with our commitment to

sustainability on top of our mind. Our Heiploeg facility in Zoutkamp

expanded and improved facility. The new design combines innovation

in logistics, automations and architecture to affect a lot more energy

efficiency. We’ve also commissioned two new ships, both designed in a

more fuel efficient manner. The first one, the Jan Maria, was launched at

the beginning of 2023 and will be christened later this year.

Our memberships in organizations such as the Pelagic Freezer-trawler

Association (PFA), Europêche, Orthongel, and AIPCE-CEP reflect and

help realize our commitment to industry-wide sustainability efforts.

As a global leader in the fish and seafood industry, we recognize our

responsibility to collaborate with partners and work towards shared

## goals.

I am immensely proud of our employees across 15 countries, who

demonstrate steadfast passion and dedication to positive change within

our industry. We are committed to creating a safe and nurturing work

environment for all our employees while embracing the global

challenges ahead. That’s why I enthusiastically share our 2022 CSR report

## with you.

## fish, for life.

## Where do we come from?

## Parlevliet & Van der Plas, founded 1950

Katwijk residents Dirk Parlevliet and brothers Dirk and Jan van der Plas began their herring business

with just 50 Guilders and an old lorry. They bought herring at the Katwijk herring auction and

sold them successfully in the domestic market. Soon, they invested in a boat and started catching

herring themselves. Their first trawler, ‘Jan Maria,’ began operating in 1959, and their first freezer

trawler, ‘Annie Hillina,’ followed in 1967. The company initially focused on pelagic species such as

herring, horse mackerel, mackerel, and blue whiting.

PP Group’s growth started in the 80 s and 90 s with the acquisition of Doggerbank Seefischerei in

1986. The group continued to expand by acquiring Mecklenburger Hochseefischerei (MHF) and

founding German Seafrozen Fish (GSF). The MHF takeover also led to PP Group’s involvement

in fishing for demersal species such as cod, redfish, and halibut. GSF specializes in the sale of

demersal fish species produced by the group. In 2003, PP Group launched Euro Baltic Fisch.

PP Group took its first step into processing consumer products with the acquisition of Ouwehand

Visverwerking in 2009, followed by the acquisition of Heiploeg International and Deutsche See, both

fish and seafood processors. Deutsche See is also a leading company specialized in the entire fish

and seafood range for retail, wholesale, and top-class catering.

PP Group established a base in France and acquired major tuna supplier Compagnie Française

du Thon Oceanique (CFTO) in 2016. The group has shareholdings (not majority owned) in France,

Spain, Portugal, Poland, the United Kingdom, Madagascar, Lithuania, Latvia, the Faroe Islands, and

Ivory Coast. Since 2002, PP Group’s head office has been located in Valkenburg, near Katwijk, the

home of both the Parlevliet and Van der Plas families.

## The state of the world

Globally and across various sectors, climate change has emerged as a widely recognized phenomenon,

urging us to reconsider our business practices. The effects of climate change are increasingly evident,

and solutions can no longer be deferred. The climate agreement outlines explicit ambitions, but

immediate action from all sectors and industries is crucial. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate

Change (IPCC) reports and global observations indicate an urgent need for drastic measures to

mitigate climate change (to preferable no more than 1.5 °C change in temperature). Companies must

accept and fulfill their responsibilities, minimizing their footprint and reducing GHG emissions.

Not only companies, but also governments, are catching up. To match ambitions with rules and

regulations. Soon the CSRD will be applicable - for which our CSR report is a good start to report on our

## sustainability risks, efforts and goals.

For PP Group, sustainability is woven into our vision. Our oceans make earth habitable, serving as an

essential resource that we must carefully manage to protect our planet, its inhabitants, and combat

climate change. Oceans supply over 3 billion people with affordable, nutritious food, making it

imperative to safeguard and sustainably manage marine ecosystems and fish stocks for optimal yields.

Although most fish consumed today come from sustainably managed stocks, there is potential

for further improvement. Numerous challenges still threaten ocean health. Additionally, the rising

global population increases the demand for healthy and nutritious fish and seafood products.

We must go beyond current sustainable fishing practices, refining the standards and criteria that

define sustainability. We believe our knowledge and experience can contribute significantly to the

development of fishing and certification standards. Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) fishing

continues to grow globally, negatively impacting fish stocks. PP Group actively opposes this trend,

striving to maintain healthy fish stocks. Alongside substantial by-catch, this poses a direct threat to

many species. We are eager to collaborate with multiple stakeholders to identify and implement

solutions, which we have been doing for many years. Furthermore, addressing plastic pollution in

our oceans and improving the wellbeing and quality of life in coastal communities present numerous

opportunities for collective progress toward a more sustainable industry. At PP Group, we commit to

driving improvements in our sector, aiming to preserve the health of both fish stocks and our oceans.

By recognizing our role and exerting our efforts, we can contribute to the establishment of healthy and

sustainable fish and seafood systems worldwide. We consider this essential for maintaining the health

of our oceans and the planet, as well as meeting the demands of consumers who are progressively

## seeking sustainable products.

## Our mission

PP Group aspires to be a globally recognized supplier of premium quality, nutritious, and sustainable

fish and seafood products. With our roots as a fish and seafood company since 1950, our goal is to

responsibly manage and protect marine and coastal ecosystems from pollution while preserving

the fish stocks essential for maintaining a healthy ecosystem. We focus on utilizing only the surplus

production, or as we like to put it: safeguarding the capital in the sea and using the interest solely. This

approach not only helps us address some of the challenges faced by our oceans, but also enables us

to supply the world’s growing population with healthy and innovative fish and seafood products for

## both present and future generations.

## CSR Strategy and Programs

In recent years, we have defined a clear CSR strategy and programs, complete with goals and focus

areas. Our three pillars form a robust foundation for a sustainable future. As a group, made up of

several companies with distinct products and core processes, we acknowledge that focus may vary

between our companies. As a holding company, we will not prescribe in detail how our portfolio

companies should develop their specific procedures and policies. Instead, we embrace diversity and

promote our companies to adapt this group CSR strategy to their own tailored CSR programs.

## Taking care of our oceans and marine life

## in a responsible manner is a fundamental

## part of running a sustainable business for

## us. We deeply believe that human welfare is

intrinsically connected to the health, protection,

## and preservation of our most crucial marine

## ecosystems. As such, we endeavor to maintain

## marine ecosystems and, when necessary, restore

## them without inflicting irreversible harm. To

## accomplish this, we must track, monitor, and

## assess our approach to the perception, handling,

and management of oceans and marine resources. Our expertise, experience,

and resources are invaluable tools in our quest to enhance industry standards for

sustainable fisheries whenever opportunities present themselves. For instance,

collaborating with partners and the broader industry to address key issues will directly

connect our contributions to the Sustainable Development Goal that relates the most

to our core values and business: SDG 14 Life below water.

We see many positive developments and initiatives that are protecting our oceans.

Vulnerable marine areas are increasingly protected and utilized responsibly; policies

promoting the responsible use of resources are in place; and people worldwide are

endorsing such projects. The strain on our marine yields has nearly halved between

2003 and 2018. As a result, up to 47% of all stocks now meet environmental criteria,

ensuring that stock exploitation and reproductive capacity align with their maximum

sustainable yield (MSY). As PP Group, we want to see this trend to continue. That’s

why our strategy encompasses the ambition to sustainably use and protect ocean

resources and marine ecosystems, thus preserving marine biodiversity. We also strive

to further develop industry and product-specific standards related to sustainability

and contribute to research promoting sustainable fisheries. All these efforts will

positively impact biodiversity and help limit and mitigate the effects of climate change

on our oceans and planet. Through our strategy, we support life both below and above

water. For more information, please refer to the chapter Our achievements for a happy

## planet in our report.

## employed within our industry to determine the

safe and healthy size of fish stocks. The maximum

## sustainable yield represents the highest possible

## annual catch that can be sustained over time,

## maintaining the stock at a level that produces

## maximum growth. This approach enables

## long-term fishing without damaging fish stocks.

## Marine Stewardship Council

## The Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) is an

## independent organization that employs a

## science-based certification method to evaluate

## and certify wild fish and seafood products. By

## involving stakeholders in the process, MSC aims

## to enhance the credibility of their standards

## for sustainable fishing and traceable seafood

## while collaboratively improving the fishing

## industry, step by step. Under the MSC standard

## requirements, fisheries are mandated to

## continuously improve until they reach what is

## considered the best practice in sustainability.

## Aquaculture Stewardship Council

## The Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC)

## is the world’s leading certification scheme for

## responsibly farmed seafood. The ASC label is

## only found on products from farms that have

## been independently assessed and certified as

## environmentally and socially responsible. ASC

## develops and manages the strictest standards in

## the industry. To achieve ASC certification, farms

## undergo independent assessments against

## these stringent requirements. These standards

## focus on water quality, responsible sourcing

## of feed, disease prevention, animal welfare,

## fair treatment and compensation of workers,

## and maintaining positive relationships with

## surrounding communities.

## GlobalG.A.P.

## GlobalG.A.P. is an internationally recognized

## organization that develops comprehensive

## standards for Good Agricultural Practices

## across various sectors, including aquaculture. It

## addresses key aspects of sustainable production,

## such as environmental protection, animal

## welfare, food safety, worker health and safety,

## social compliance, and traceability. By adhering

## to these standards, producers can demonstrate

## their commitment to responsible and sustainable

## practices, gaining certification that enhances

## market access, consumer trust, and promotes

## positive change within the aquaculture industry.

## Steps to more sustainable fishing practices

PP Group strives to ensure that all fisheries where our vessels operate achieve certification,

such as the MSC. It has established guidelines and standards to promote sustainable fishing

practices. We are committed to complying with these rules and standards to maintain our MSC

certification. These standards allow third parties to evaluate fisheries on sustainability and fish

## stock management.

MSC collaborates with the fishing industry, scientists, conservation organizations, governments,

and other stakeholders to develop and review these standards. The goal is to transform the global

fish and seafood markets. The assessment process is similar for nearly all fish and shellfish species.

MSC evaluates companies based on three key principles:

## Sustainable fish stocks

Fishing must be done at a level that allows fishing indefinitely and keeps fish stocks

## productive and healthy.

## Minimize impact on other marine life

Fisheries must operate carefully to preserve the health of other marine life and their

## habitats.

## Effective fisheries management

MSC-certified fisheries must comply with applicable laws and be able to adapt to changing

## environmental conditions affecting fish stocks.

PP Group aims to use its influence to not only increase the share of certified products but

also collaborate with industry stakeholders to make worldwide sustainable and healthy stock

management a reality. Our ambitions go beyond certification alone. We are proud to work with

internationally recognized and independent certification bodies, like MSC, that support our

## mission.

We adhere strictly to the guidelines and principles set by these certification bodies and have limited

influence on certifications and related processes. Our focus is on understanding and incorporating

the shared standards, while recognizing that certifications may be withdrawn by the relevant

authorities based on regulatory or environmental developments.

## Fish Aggregating Devices (FADs)

In our tuna fishing activities, we utilize FADs (Fish

## Aggregating Devices) to attract tuna species.

## These man-made devices, equipped with a GPS

## localization buoy, are placed in the water to

## aggregate fish for effective catching strategies.

## FADs are deployed by purse seine or support

vessels and drift at sea to attract small fish.

## Larger predators, such as tuna, gather beneath

the FADs, drawn by the smaller prey, and are

## then harvested.

## Although efforts are made to prevent it within

the industry, FADs can be abandoned or lost at

sea, posing a risk of marine pollution. At CFTO,

we are dedicated to solve this marine issue by

## closely examining how to prevent or retrieve

FADs left at sea. This is done by taking part in

## FAD watch - a collaboration of organizations

## in reducing FAD impact. Through FAD Watch -

## crews are supported to retrieve FADs. Since 2022

the Seychelle Fishing Authorities are a partner in

## helping retrieve FADs. Upon retrieval, FADs are

## reused and/or recycled

## Our tuna company, CFTO, is one of the

## organizations working to reduce the negative

## impact and pollution of FADs. Since 2012, CFTO

has set limits on the number of FADs deployed.

## CFTO uses 100% non-entangling FADs to prevent

## harm to species and recovers FADs before they

## beach or approach corals too closely.

• sustainability with reference and management of marine fisheries

fishing, fisheries management, safety at sea and data collection

## Partnership with Oceans Unlocked

Since 2021 we capture more of the vast potential of the high seas, by collaborating through the

## joint venture; Oceans Unlocked.

Through this collaboration we aim to offer healthy ocean derived ingredients for food,

pharmaceutical and cosmetic industries. This by sustainably sourcing more and different

sources than PP Group has traditionally done (until now), like:

1. Cultivating nutritional seaweed in dedicated farms - without the need of fresh water,

## rich soil, fertilizer or land

2. Processing and distributing seaweed for food, binding and bioplastic products

3. Utilizing rest streams from the fish and shrimp catches and processing, for pharma

and cosmetic companies (mainly collagen for ‘nutraceuticals’)

Part of the Oceans Unlocked company are the subsidiaries Green Marine Farming, Seaflavours

and Bluewave Biotech; that take up these respective activities. There is a huge potential for

these industries. In Asia seaweed is already an important ingredient for many food products.

Seaweed can locally extract CO 2 (carbonate) from the water, decreasing acidification. Also it ads

livable space for different kinds of species - contributing to local biodiversity.

Collagen is already a 4 Billion Euro market, to which we can add a necessary sustainable source

that’s affordable. Now it’s mostly gained from animals in Asia, to be used in Western markets. Our

processes can more regionally add a sustainable source of collagen to western collagen markets.

## Challenges:

There are still some significant challenges in these endeavors. There is still a lot of energy

needed for the processing of fishing rest streams. But with the insecurity on the energy markets,

this poses a big risk. Another challenge is the decentralized nature of all the vessels. All leftovers

from filleting of cod, salmon and tuna are now distributed waste over dozens of vessels. How

do we get those reststreams centralized in an affordable way. Besides - how is this biological

material, that is now classified as ‘waste’, properly processed and stored. So it can pragmatically

and legally still be considered food (quality) to use in our products.

One way to tackle these problems is by working at the processing location of TK Fish, the

Heiploeg International subsidiary. Here we have the challenge of repurposing 300 containers of

shrimp shell ‘waste’ to 4 containers of nutraceutical that can be imported in the EU. While still

applying all EU regulations concerning food and product safety.
