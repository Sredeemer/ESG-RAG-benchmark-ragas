Throughout 2021, Boardwalk continued to deliver on our mission to create value for our stakeholders through superior customer service and a commitment to operational excellence. Our mission supports our vision of being a leading provider of energy services while providing safe and reliable infrastructure throughout the thirteen states where we operate.

We remain focused on advancing our sustainability strategy through our commitment to environmental stewardship and safety, investing in our people and our communities and maintaining a strong culture of governance and ethics.

The demand for our services is increasing and we expect the demand for natural gas to continue to increase due to the growth of LNG exports to Europe and Asia and the growth in natural gas fired electric generation prompted by ongoing coal retirements. The disruptions to energy supply experienced throughout the country continue to highlight the need for increased resiliency of the grid and the important position natural gas holds in a lower carbon future.

To meet this growing demand, natural gas infrastructure needs to expand. We are prepared to meet these growth challenges as an industry while also doing our part to reduce the environmental impact and emissions associated with our assets. We are advancing our GHG emissions reduction program, which includes new projects dedicated to decreasing our methane emissions. As a result of prioritizing our emissions reduction program, Boardwalk has reduced our methane emissions by 31% since 2020.

We are continuing to evaluate emerging technologies such as hydrogen, carbon capture and storage, renewable natural gas and waste heat-to-power. We are investing in renewable energy pilot projects such as installing solar panels at compressor stations and meters and adding electric vehicles to our company fleet. We understand the importance of collaborating with others across our industry and continue to be a part of the conversation through numerous industry associations. It is also imperative that we continue maintaining our existing infrastructure so that natural gas can continue to play a vital role in the reduction of GHG emissions.

Our pipelines run through both rural and urban areas, including adjacent to my own residence. We owe all our constituents, our employees, the communities in which our facilities operate, our customers and investors, a commitment to safety and reliability. In 2021, Boardwalk experienced its lowest Total Recordable Incident Rate in over 5 years, and we are strengthening our approach to safety and reliability through our Pipeline Safety Management System. Our employees continue to hold safety top of mind every day. I would like to thank our employees for making it possible for us to provide reliable service to our customers, including during various weather events such as Winter Storm Uri as well as hurricanes and tropical storms.

Our employees’ dedication is critical to advancing our sustainability strategy. As the energy industry evolves, we recognize the importance of hiring and retaining excellent employees to support our efforts. The pandemic brought an increased focus on work-life balance, and we have adopted new work schedules that provide employees more flexibility in how they work. We are focused on developing initiatives that enhance the employee experience and encourage inclusivity and diversity of thought. In 2022, Boardwalk enhanced its parental leave benefits for employees following the birth of an employee’s child or the placement of a child with an employee in connection with a legal adoption. We recognize that our people are and will continue to be our greatest asset, and we must grow and develop our employees as well as bring new and diverse talent into our industry.

We also value giving back to the communities we serve through our charitable giving and volunteer efforts. In 2021, we donated to over 273 organizations through our matching programs and corporate donations. We value engaging with the various stakeholder groups within our communities including farmers, ranchers, landowners, first responders, local governments and others who live near our operations. When beginning new projects in any community, we seek to facilitate open and meaningful dialogue that provides everyone with an opportunity to participate in the discussions. The formation of our Environmental Justice Committee furthers this effort by improving outreach and dialogue with those individuals that live in and around our operating areas who may be underrepresented, disadvantaged or subject to a disproportionate burden of environmental impacts.

I am proud to share this Sustainability Report, which provides more detail on the efforts Boardwalk has undertaken throughout 2021 to continue improving our record of being a good corporate citizen. This report is our opportunity to capture some of the ways that Boardwalk and our employees are adapting to a changing energy landscape and highlight how we plan to continue making progress towards our business goals and sustainability initiatives. We remain dedicated to providing safe, reliable and affordable energy and look forward to our industry’s bright future.

Stan Horton, President and CEO, Boardwalk Pipelines, LP

Industry Associations Boardwalk is engaged with peers in the energy sector on best practices for the responsible development of natural resources. Maintaining a dialogue with peer companies creates opportunities for information exchange, including sharing technological developments and innovative solutions that will advance the industry’s collective mission to ensure safe operations with minimal environmental impact. Participating in association activities also allows us a means of effective communication with government agency representatives and academic researchers seeking to better understand the challenges faced by the industry.

We participate in several associations at the national level:

Energy Infrastructure Council (EIC): EIC is a non-profit trade association dedicated to advancing the interests of companies that develop and operate energy infrastructure. EIC addresses core public policy issues critical to investment in America’s energy infrastructure. Our CEO serves on the Board of EIC.

Interstate Natural Gas Association of America (INGAA): INGAA is a trade organization that advocates regulatory and legislative positions of importance to the natural gas pipeline industry in North America. Boardwalk continues to be an active member of INGAA, building on a history of leadership within the organization including a member of our senior management team previously serving as Chair of the INGAA Board of Directors and currently serving as a member of the INGAA Board and Steering Council. In 2021, Boardwalk employees continued their engagement with several INGAA Committees including the Environmental Committee and task forces created to address emissions-related issues and the Pipeline Safety Committee. Boardwalk is also a member of the INGAA Foundation, which is focused on sponsoring research supporting the safe, reliable, and environmentally responsible operation of interstate natural gas transmission pipelines. The Foundation organizes studies and educational conferences on issues of importance like environmental impacts of energy production and use, improvements in construction practices, and new approaches to identifying and minimizing emissions. In 2021, a Boardwalk Vice President began a two-year term on the INGAA Foundation’s Executive Committee.

Our Nation’s Energy (ONE) Future Coalition (ONE Future): The ONE Future Coalition is a group of more than fifty natural gas companies working together to voluntarily reduce methane emissions across the natural gas value chain. As a member of ONE Future, Boardwalk supports the goal to drive reductions in methane emissions and ensure measurable results using a uniform reporting protocol. ONE Future has established an industry-wide goal of lowering methane emissions to less than 1% of total natural gas produced additively across the value chain (also known as “methane intensity”) by 2025. For the transmission and storage portion of the natural gas value chain, ONE Future set a target of 0.30% methane intensity by 2025. A member of Boardwalk’s leadership team currently serves on ONE Future’s Board of Directors.

Pipeline Research Council International (PRCI): PRCI is a community of the world’s leading pipeline companies, and the vendors, service providers, equipment manufacturers, and other organizations supporting the industry. Since 1952, PRCI has been recognized around the world as a unique forum within the energy pipeline industry through the development and deployment of research solutions to improve pipeline safety and performance.

Southern Gas Association (SGA): The SGA is a trade association for natural gas professionals that supports all sectors of the industry. It is comprised of over 200 member organizations and links people, ideas, and information to promote a safe and sustainable energy future. Our CEO has served on the Board of Directors for a number of years. Our Senior Vice President of Operations and Engineering has been the chairperson for SGA’s Executive Council for the past two years and now sits on the SGA Board of Directors. Our Senior Vice President of Human Resources, Communications and Sustainability currently serves on SGA’s ESG and Corporate Services Section Managing Committee and the Diversity and Inclusion Council.

Our dedication to business ethics is supported by our Core Value of Integrity, which means doing the right thing, always. Strong ethics and compliance with all applicable laws and regulations are essential to our success, and we require every employee to engage in ethical behavior.

## Code of Business Conduct and Ethics

We are committed to maintaining high standards for honest and ethical conduct in the operation of the Company and all of our business dealings. Our senior management has established a Code of Business Conduct and Ethics (Code), which is available on our website for all employees and external parties to view. ## Systems and Practices for Reporting Violations

Boardwalk’s Ethics Hotline is available for all employees to anonymously report ethics violations and concerns. The hotline is managed by a third- party provider 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

Management has established and communicated an Ethics Hotline Policy to all employees. All calls into the hotline are initially reviewed by the Company’s General Counsel, and/or other members of senior management are included as needed. All calls that are received through the hotline

are confidential and are investigated using appropriate protocols, with the results communicated to various parties, as appropriate and on a confidential basis.

## Managing Climate-Related Risks

Boardwalk provides transportation and storage services for natural gas and NGLs. We generally do not buy and sell natural gas or NGLs other than for operational purposes. Changes in the commodity pricing can impact the volumes transported and stored by our customers. The majority of our revenues are fee-based, being derived from capacity reservation charges under firm transportation and storage agreements with customers, which do not vary significantly period to period, but are impacted by longer-term trends in our business markets or industry. We contract directly with end-use customers, including electric power generators, local distribution companies, industrial end users and exporters of liquified natural gas, as well as producers and marketers of natural gas, and interstate and intrastate pipelines who, in turn, provide transportation and storage services for their customers.

Our assets are critical in facilitating the end-use of the commodities for which we provide services, some of which help reduce overall global greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions when compared with the alternative.

Physical Risks Physical risks related to the physical implications of climate change could have a financial impact to an entity. Physical risks can either be acute or chronic. Acute physical risks are those risks that are event driven. These risks include increased severity and frequency of extreme weather events due to climate change, such as hurricanes, tornadoes, flooding, wintry conditions, subsidence and erosion. Chronic physical risks are those that occur due to longer-term shifts in climate patterns. These risks include sustained higher temperatures that may cause increases in sea levels or chronic heat waves. These physical risks could result in decreased system reliability and capacity while also increasing costs.

The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) has identified two types of climate-related risks: transition risks and physical risks, which are further discussed below.

Transition Risks Transition risks are those risks associated with moving towards a lower- carbon world. TCFD divides transition risks into four groups: policy and legal, technology, market, and reputation.

Policy and Legal Risks: Regulation, or policy actions, related to climate change have, and will continue to impact our business. Legal and litigation risk is also present from local governments and landowners claiming energy companies, including Boardwalk, contributed to increased coastal marsh loss and erosion and are seeking substantial damages. The potential financial impact of policy, new regulations and legal risks are increased compliance, legal and operation costs; increased costs for growth and maintenance projects and changes in demand for our transportation and storage services, which could impact operating revenues, either positively or negatively. We are also focused on adopting energy-efficiency solutions, such as installation of renewable energy sources at some locations, and promoting more sustainable land-use practices, such as using wastewater evaporators, to reduce the amount of wastewater sent to disposal facilities.

We recognize the importance of protecting both our physical assets and our information and operational control systems from threats that could disrupt our business, put our assets at risk or compromise our customer and employee data. The effective protection of our assets and technology infrastructure is crucial to the reliability of our operations, our ability to serve our customers and the nation’s energy needs, and the security of our data. We have a comprehensive strategy to address both physical and cybersecurity threats.

## Cybersecurity

We leverage technology and electronic information to conduct our business and operate our assets safely and reliably. To protect our technology infrastructure against increasingly sophisticated threats, we have a comprehensive cybersecurity program that aligns with industry practices and recognized standards and guidelines. Cybersecurity is overseen by Boardwalk’s Vice President and Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) who leads a team responsible for our technology defense posture and program.

We follow the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework, which provides guidance for how private-sector organizations can assess and improve their ability to prevent, detect, and respond to cyber-attacks. We also reference International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 27001 for certain aspects of our cybersecurity program, such as our policy, standards and guidelines framework. Under the Department of Homeland Security, we are subject to the Transportation Security Administration Security Directive 2 (SD 2) and must adhere to SD 2 requirements and measures. In addition to our regulatory obligation to adhere to these certain requirements, we independently assess our cybersecurity posture via third-party assessments such as penetration tests, purple-teaming activities, industry-specific health-checks, and point-specific technical cyber assessments of key systems. Some of these assessments are performed independently with internal audit oversight.

Our cybersecurity program also includes an incident response plan and a team dedicated to hardening our cybersecurity posture, monitoring our networks, providing training to our employees, and analyzing the evolution of new threats and mitigations. The program is regularly exercised, reviewed, updated, and vetted through third-party audits, assessments, and tests to validate its effectiveness in reducing or eliminating risk, as well as compliance with legal and regulatory requirements.

Our Chief Information Officer, CISO and other cybersecurity staff members provide holistic updates to our CEO and senior management team at least quarterly, with more frequent updates regarding specific situations such as intelligence pointing to increased adversary activity. Our CIO and CISO also attend weekly executive leadership meetings to give updates on any immediate cybersecurity threats, risks, and regulatory changes as well as any improvements or impediments to our cybersecurity posture.

We regularly review and update our Spill Reporting, Response, and Cleanup procedures that are outlined in our Environmental Procedures Manual.

## Safety Program

Safety is one of our Core Values and guides daily tasks and procedures throughout the organization. All employees are accountable and responsible for their personal safety conduct and the safety oversight of others in their work environment.

We have an extensive Safety Program that provides expectations and frameworks for operating assets in a safe, reliable and environmentally sensitive manner. Our Safety Program is aligned with relevant Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) and PHMSA requirements.

Boardwalk received the GPA Midstream First Place Safety Award for Division 1 for its 2021 safety record. The Division 1 category is for large midstream companies that have 1 million or more midstream operational workhours.

Safety Tracking and Reporting The continuous improvement of safety performance is critical to our business. We use metrics to help monitor safety performance, including recordable incidents and preventable vehicle incidents. Based on these metrics, we identify lessons learned and implement corrective actions to avoid future incidents.

Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR) We review all incidents, including near misses, and conduct investigations to determine if there is a need for changes in our protocols or if enhanced training should be made available. During the 2019-2021 period, Boardwalk did not experience an OSHA workplace injury classified as an amputation, loss of sight, loss of consciousness, respiratory illness, or death.

Preventable Vehicle Incident Rate Motor vehicle safety is a focus area as we seek to eliminate preventable motor incidents. Preventable vehicle incident rate is an indicator of employee driving performance and helps manage risks associated with driving. Boardwalk’s Preventable Vehicle Incident Rate for 2021 was 1.58.

Fleet vehicle drivers are provided instructor lead Smith System driver training that includes a written performance evaluation and skills recommendations to practice that develop safe driving habits. Drivers are also required to perform a 360-degree vehicle safety inspection prior to operating. The employee-led Safety Advisory Team collaborated with Fleet Management to enhance vehicle braking visibility by installing rear LED brake light strips on fleet vehicles. Fleet Management also upgraded vehicle purchasing guidelines to include collision avoidance systems on fleet vehicles.
