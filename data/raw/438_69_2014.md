Merck is a leading company for innovative, top- quality high-tech products in the healthcare, life science and performance materials sectors.

Around 39,000 em- ployees across 66 countries work to im- prove quality of life for patients, to foster the success of customers, and to help meet global challenges such as climate change and access barriers.

Merck is the world’s oldest pharmaceutical and chemical company. Since 1668 our name has stood for innova- tion, business success and responsible entrepreneur- ship. The founding family remains the majority owner of the company to this day.

We are Merck, the original, and hold the global rights to the Merck name and brand. The only exceptions are Canada and the United States, where we operate as EMD Serono, EMD Millipore and EMD Performance Materials.

## We have three business sectors:

Healthcare develops and produces prescription medicines to treat conditions such as cancer, infertility or multiple sclerosis; over-the-counter pharmaceutical products for the relief of colds and pain; and innovations in the allergy and biosimilars fields.

The challenge Approximately 249 million people worldwide suffer from the worm disease schistosomiasis, with more than 90% of those affected living in sub-Saharan Africa. This chronic parasitic condition is one of the most devastating neglected tropical diseases (NTDs) in terms of public health burden and economic impact.

How we‘re helping Since 2007, Merck has been partnering with the World Health Organization (WHO) in the fight against schistosomiasis. Our goal is to eliminate the disease in Africa. To date, Merck has supplied WHO with more than 200 million praziquantel tablets, and over 54 million patients in total have been treated, consisting primarily of school-age children. We have pledged to increase the number of tablets donated annually to up to 250 million. On top of this, we are funding a program to teach children in African schools about schistosomiasis. Within the scope of a public-private partnership, Merck is also conducting research on a pediatric formulation of praziquantel for children under the age of six. At the end of 2014, we called for various neglected tropical disease (NTD) constituencies to form the Global Schistosomiasis Alliance in order to help eliminate this disease worldwide.

The challenge Around 70% of the one billion inhabitants of India reside in rural areas and have no access to effective, affordable health care. Medical facilities are concen- trated in India‘s megacities, which account for 80% of India‘s healthcare professionals as well as 70% of the country‘s hospital beds.

How we‘re helping Through the Suswastha Project, Merck is seeking to improve health care for rural populations of India. At community-level weekly meetings, healthcare professionals educate patients about everyday health issues such as coughs and childhood ailments, teaching them ways to prevent these conditions. In addition to this, the program also provides free checkups and offers continuing education to help local physicians advance their medical capacities. Furthermore, Merck is partnering with the non-governmental organizati- on FHI 360 as well as the Clinton Foundation to sup- port their fight against diarrheal diseases.

Since its start in January 2013, 1,675 rural villages in India have benefited from the Suswastha project, which has now reached half a million patients in total.

The challenge In low- and middle-income countries, the number of diabetes patients is constantly on the rise. In Africa, approximately 20 million people suffer from this condition. Very few patients there have adequate access to insulin, syringes, or the medical equipment needed to monitor their blood sugar.

How we‘re helping Merck is committed to improving the quality of diabetes treatment as well as raising awareness of this disease, especially in low- and middle-income countries. To help achieve this, we have partnered with ministries of health and academic institutions to institute the Capacity Advancement Program (CAP).

Since launching this program in 2012, we have hosted 250 events to boost awareness of the dangers and consequences of diabetes. On top of that, we have provided free diabetes screening and medical checkups to more than 25,000 people in Kenya, Ghana, Uganda, and India. In 2014, around 2,000 pharmacy and medical students in Africa took part in clinical diabetes management training.

The challenge The danger of serious climate damage has increased in recent years, while many natural resources have continued to grow scarcer. As a result, people are becoming more environmentally conscious, and con- sumers are increasingly taking a product‘s power rating into account when deciding what to buy.

How we‘re helping Our PS-VA liquid crystal technology (polymer- stabilized vertical alignment) provides devices such as television screens with high contrast while keeping energy consumption low. They furthermore increase light transmittance, which helps to significantly reduce the amount of backlighting needed - the largest power consumer in a display. In comparison to VA technology, screens with Merck‘s PS-VA materials thus consume up to 20% less energy.

Our UB-FFS technology (ultra-brightness fringe field switching) was recognized with the German Inno- vation Award in April 2015. This is the first technolo- gy to enable energy-efficient liquid crystal touch- screens for smartphones and tablets that glow brightly and have a long battery life. UB-FFS utilizes the back- lighting more fully, thereby lowering end device power consumption by 30%.

The challenge In the West, 40% of total energy consumption is attributable to buildings, 20% of which goes to lighting and 15% to air conditioning. With resources becoming ever scarcer and energy prices rising ever higher, companies and private individuals are seeking to boost the energy efficiency of both new buildings as well as existing ones.

How we‘re helping We are working with architects, glass makers and facade manufacturers to create the windows of tomorrow. Our ambitious goal is to use smart windows to make buildings more energy-efficient, thus con- serving resources and cutting costs. To this end, we have developed an innovative liquid crystal techno- logy that allows windows to be darkened at the touch of a button. Here, the liquid crystals are combined with customized dyes. When a low voltage is applied to the windows, the LCs allow electromagnetic waves, i.e. light, to either pass through, thus making the window transparent, or to be absorbed and blocked, thus darkening the window. In this way, smart win- dows help regulate the temperature indoors while also lowering HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air- conditioning) costs.

The challenge Our customers are also striving to conserve resources and protect the environment, which means they expect products from Merck that help them minimize their own environmental impacts.

How we’re helping Merck Millipore has developed the Design for Sus- tainability (Df S) program, which aims to reduce the environmental impacts of our products across their entire life cycle, from manufacture to end of life. Group-wide procurement guidelines To complement our Group Procurement Policy, we drafted our Responsible Sourcing Principles in 2013 and then integrated them into our general terms and conditions in 2014. They therefore constitute the foundation of every sourcing transaction and proce- dure at Merck. In addition to requiring adherence to these principles, we also obligate our suppliers to apply our corporate responsibility standards to their upstream value chain. Furthermore, Merck also collects self-reported supplier information and conducts CR audits on specific vendors based on the potential risk they pose. To this end, we assign each supplier to one of three risk categories, based on the risk levels in their country, the product category, and the share of their sales that come from Merck. In general, we assign vendors from non-OECD countries to a higher risk category. Furthermore, suppliers are also audited if there are indications that they have failed to comply with our requirements. In response to the results of our risk assessment, a total of 49 CR audits were conducted in 2013 and 2014, either by us or by a service provider.

Together for Sustainability At the end of 2014, Merck joined the Together for Sustainability industry initiative, which is dedicated to improving the supply chain with respect to en- vironmental, compliance and social standards. The objective of this network, which was established in 2011, is to harmonize supplier assessments and audits worldwide and to further the responsible procure- ment of goods and services. The evaluation results are utilized jointly by member companies, who abide by all restrictions stipulated in competition law.

International supply chain We source the raw materials, packaging materials, technical products, components, and services we need from over 60,000 suppliers across more than 130 countries. All goods and services purchased in 2013 totaled € 4.1 billion, with € 4.3 billion in 2014. Of the goods and services (including R&D services) purchased in 2014, 55% came from vendors based in EU countries and 30% from vendors in OECD countries outside the EU. The share of goods and services sourced from non-OECD countries outside the EU rose from 12% in 2012 to 15% in 2014.

Growing importance of emerging markets Merck is increasingly sourcing goods and services from emerging markets. We continuously check whether suppliers located in these areas are adhering to our standards. Emerging countries are growing more important as sourcing markets in part because we produce certain pharmaceutical products in the markets where they are commercialized - such as India, Pakistan and Indonesia. This approach reflects our Access to Health strategy and is also stipulated by local law. We manufacture all our pharmaceutical products - whether for developed or developing markets - in manufacturing plants that meet global Good Manufacturing Practices (GMPs).

Environmental management system Our EHS policy (Environment, Health & Safety) is implemented through internal guidelines and in- struction manuals. In addition to this, local EHS managers are in charge of operational environmental protection at each of our individual sites. We have internal and external audits conducted on a regular basis to determine whether our environmental man- agement processes are still meeting ISO 14001 requirements. In 2014, Merck received the group certificate for its environ- mental management system for the sixth con- secutive year.

Plant and process safety One of our top goals is to prevent production disruptions, which could lead to workplace acci- dents and chemical spillage. Our Group-wide standard “Plant and Process Safety” mandates safety-related require- ments to be applied across the entire life cycle of a plant - meaning construction, normal operation, modifications, maintenance, repair, and shutdown.

Energy efficiency and carbon emissions Climate change and its consequences are one of the main challenges facing society in the 21 st century – and it goes without saying that we will do our part to overcome these issues. We have therefore set our- selves the goal of reducing total direct and indirect greenhouse gas emissions by 20% by 2020, relative to the 2006 baseline. In order to achieve this goal, Merck has initiated a climate protection program called EDISON that consolidates all climate change

mitigation and energy efficiency activities of the Merck Group. Worldwide, 20 Merck sites jointly account for 80% of our greenhouse gas emissions, so EDISON is focusing on these sites in particular. In 2014, Merck lowered its greenhouse gas emissions by around 8% relative to the 2006 baseline, despite growth in its operating business.

Water In our production processes, we use water primarily for cooling, as process water, or for exhaust air puri- fication. After being utilized, the water may contain substances such as heavy metals or active pharma- ceutical ingredients. The treatment of this wastewater, and thus the prevention of environmental pollution, is therefore a fundamental part of our environmental protection activities worldwide. In the 2013-2014 period, we examined our sites to determine which ones are located in areas of high water stress and consume more than 30,000 cubic meters of water per year. Based on this assessment, we will be placing a greater focus on sustainable water management across multiple sites.

Waste management We continuously work to optimize our processes in order to divert, reduce and recycle waste. For in- stance, in the 2013-2014 period we implemented a recycling process for heptane, a chemical that accumulates during the production of liquid crystals. We collect the heptane and have an external recy- cling company treat it. Our recycling rate is over 85%. We then reuse the purified solvent in our processes.

In November 2014, Merck commissioned a carbon- neutral biomass power plant in Goa, India.

Focus of our involvement Our social responsibility activities are primarily focused on areas in which we have specific problem solving expertise stemming from our businesses. We therefore engage in health promotion projects and environmental protection, while also fostering cultural activities and educational initiatives. We provide disaster relief in emergency situations, especially in those regions in which we operate. In 2014, Merck invested a total of € 50.8 million in community involvement.

Global projects To maximize the effectiveness and impact of our projects, we concentrate our resources on three global projects:

In the Merck Praziquantel Donation Program, we are partnering with the World Health Organization (WHO) to combat the worm disease schistosomia- sis in African school children (see page 8 for more information).

Interpol estimates that up to 30% of all medicines in emerging and developing nations are counter- feit. The GPHF, a non-profit organization funded by Merck, has developed a compact mobile labo- ratory known as the Minilab. With this kit, counterfeit medicines can be detected quickly, easily and cheaply. Reference samples are used to test the identity and concentration of 75 active ingredients in total, ranging from anti-malarials and antibiotics to analgesics and antipyretics. To date, the GPHF has supplied 685 Minilabs across 90 countries at cost.

The Deutsche Philharmonie Merck is the musical ambassador of our company. More than 26,000 people attend our professional orchestra’s

concerts each year. In addition to this, the Deutsche Philharmonie Merck regularly invites international ensembles to Darmstadt and itself also tours the globe. In October 2014 the orchestra gave a charity concert in the United Arab Emirates to benefit patients with multiple sclerosis (see page 12 for more information).

Global involvement Our subsidiaries are also engaged in local projects and invest in the communities in which we operate. Health, environment and culture are the key areas of our community involvement at both the global and local level. Sustainable engagement means that projects have a long-term scope, with us generally overseeing and supporting them over a period of multiple years. Our local subsidiaries were involved in 136 projects in 2014. Take for example the Erbitux® China Patients Aid Program (ECPAP) launched in 2012. In this program, we work with the Beijing Red Cross Foundation to provide Erbitux® free-of-charge to low-income patients with colorectal cancer. Over 3,100 patients have participated in the program to date.

Disaster relief In the 2013-2014 period, we extensively revamped our strategic disaster relief processes and forged a new approach. Our Group function “Public Affairs & Corporate Responsibility” works with representatives from our businesses and subsidiaries to direct our efforts to help disaster victims. In October 2014, Merck donated € 250,000 to the Deutsche Rote Kreuz (DRK) to help combat ebola.

Materiality analysis We utilize materiality along with stakeholder input to inform our CR Report, which adheres to the principles of completeness. We conducted a materiality analysis in order to surface the non-financial topics that are relevant to both our stakeholders as well as to Merck’s long-term business success. This process involved identifying the material issues, prioritizing them, and having our CR Committee validate them.

Comprehensive stakeholder surveys First we identified current CR issues for the pharma- ceutical and chemical industries. We used various sources to do so, including our experience from previous materiality analyses and CR reports, as well as the healthcare sector standards of the Sustainabil- ity Accounting Standards Board, analyses of capital market requirements, and our dialogues with various stakeholders. We then discussed these issues internally.

The next step involved an international online survey of several of our stakeholder groups, including rating agencies, suppliers, and Merck employees involved in corporate responsibility, as well as representatives from the political arena, science and research, non- governmental organizations, and business federations. We asked participants to rank how relevant the issues are to Merck, as well as to rate our performance with regard to them. In particular, participants ranked product responsibility as being especially important, overwhelmingly rating Merck‘s performance in this area as good to very good. Combined with other analysis results, the stakeholders‘ responses showed us what they think of Merck‘s CR performance, as well as which aspects they consider material to Merck. During a materiality workshop, representatives from the CR Committee validated the prioritized issues, discussed the strengths and weaknesses of the systems Merck uses to manage issues, and identified action areas for Merck. We have derived the focus of our CR report from the results of the materiality analysis.

The Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) is an independent not-for-profit organization that, on behalf of investors, purchasers and governments, has pioneered a dis- closure system enabling companies to transparently report their greenhouse gas emissions and water use. In the Carbon Disclosure Leadership Index, which assesses the level of reporting detail as well as transparency, Merck scored 87 out of 100 possible points. In the Carbon Disclosure Performance Index, which measures corporate performance in reducing emissions, Merck was rated B on a scale of E to A, which clearly places us in the upper performance band among all participating companies from Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

Since 2008, Merck has been included in the FTSE 4 Good Index, a leading inter- national sustainability index that annually evaluates companies‘ social, environ- mental and ethical performance.

In 2013, oekom research AG, a sustainability ratings agency, gave Merck a C+ on a scale of D- to A+ (A+ being the best grade). Merck thus once more achieved Prime Status.
