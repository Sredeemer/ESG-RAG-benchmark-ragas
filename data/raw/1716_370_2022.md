Danish Agro is an independent cooperative-based company owned by 8,250 Danish farmers. Danish Agro was founded in 1901 and today it is one of the largest and most significant agribusiness groups in Northern Europe. The Danish Agro group is an international agricultural group with over 5,000 employees in 15 countries, primarily in Scandinavia and the Baltic region.

Danish Agro is a member of FEFAC, DAKOFO which is the trade organisation for crop and feed trading in Denmark and is also a member of the trade associa- tion Danish Agriculture and Food Council. The group mainly deals with the selling of feed mixes, ingredi- ents and vitamin mixes, fertiliser, plant protection, seeds and energy plus the purchase of crops from farms. In addition, the group represents a number of strong machinery brands for agriculture and operates an extensive chain of hobby and leisure retail stores.

This report covers a number of policies. These poli- cies have all been adopted by the entire Danish Agro group and thus also apply to all subsidiaries.

This report is the Danish Agro group’s seventh Com- munication on Progress report and serves as the group’s mandatory report on Corporate and Social Responsibility (CSR) pursuant to Section 99(ab) of the Danish Financial Statements Act for the financial year 2022. The Danish Agro group’s annual report will explain the group’s policy on data ethics in accor- dance with Section 99(d) of the Danish Financial Sta- tements Act. The annual report will be available on Danish Agro’s website danishagro.com.

The Danish Agro group’s approach towards CSR is anchored in a CSR policy approved by the group’s management team.

FOCUS ON SUSTAINABILITY In the Danish Agro group, we endeavour to live up to our social responsibility by running our business pro- fitably and sustainably and by integrating financial, social, environmental and ethical considerations into our production and processes.

We wish to ensure a sustainable development of our business activities, and put extra emphasis on priori- tising high-quality products, high delivery reliability, limited consumption of resources and consistent sustainable development throughout our value chain and in dialogue with our stakeholders.

Danish Agro has signed the UN Global Compact and fully supports the UN guidelines for human rights and commerce. We make every effort to integrate these principles in our business and value chain based on the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the UN Global Compact’s 10 principles and a code of conduct for suppliers.

In addition, Danish Agro shares the Danish Agricul- ture and Food Council’s vision of climate-neutral foodstuffs by 2050 - which means that by that date the foodstuffs sector shall not emit more greenhouse gases than it captures.

In 2020, we started the process of developing a num- ber of specific action plans and targets to ensure that we contribute to achieving this vision. This will be through our own initiatives plus partnerships with farmers, organisations and companies, and we will need to find a financially viable route to climate-neu- tral production of foodstuffs.

In 2022, the Danish Agro board of directors has approved the Group’s new ESG targets, including interim targets towards a carbon-neutral production by 2050, i.e. by 2026 we will have reduced CO 2 e emis- sions in Scope 1 and 2 by at least 30% compared to 2020, and by 2030 the reduction will be at least 50%.

Maintaining our high employee satisfaction is also part of the Group’s new ESG targets. In 2022, we conducted an engagement and satisfaction survey globally for the first time and the objective is to maintain or improve this score by 2030.

Finally, the Group aims to promote gender under- representation in our governing bodies as well as in our leadership teams. Internally in the Group, we have 19% women at C-level (CEO, CFO and COO). Our objective is to raise this number to 30% by 2030.

In addition, in 2022 Danish Agro participated in a GUDP project called RAPS, which aims to develop a new rapeseed product that can be used in compound feed for pigs for slaughter as a local protein raw material, and is thus part of our work to find alterna- tives to soy as a protein product in pig feed. Through a collaboration across Danish Agro, Chr. Hansen, DSM and Aarhus University, it will be investigated how rapeseed cakes and rapeseed hulls can achieve a better protein and phosphorus digestibility in pigs for slaughter.

The essence of the project is to make the rapeseed products easier to digest for the pigs, as this will lead to a better feed utilisation. This will also benefit the climate by reducing nitrogen and phosphorus emis- sions in the slurry from the pigs.

In a further GUDP project, Ento Feed, Danish Agro group’s subsidiaries Hedegaard and Vilomix colla- borated with Aarhus University in 2022 to develop the production of insects as an alternative protein source in animal feed for pigs and poultry. The pro- ject works to optimise feed and feed utilisation and increase the growth of Black Soldier Fly larvae. The aim is to develop a feedstuff table and establish stan- dards for, for example, amino acid and carbohydrate levels for the larvae.

In 2020, Danish Agro, DLG and DLF joined forces to establish a new protein plant to produce air-efficient protein, and the Bio Refine protein plant was inau- gurated in 2021. At the protein plant, organic green protein is extracted from 3,000 hectares of locally produced clover grass and lucerne, and it is expected to produce about 7,000 tons of organic protein annu- ally for animal feed once the plant is fully run-in. Work on this has continued in 2022.

The plant is thus part of the work to develop locally produced protein sources and replace part of the soya that agriculture uses in feed today. It is one of northern Europe’s first to produce protein with a strong environmental and climate profile and will potentially also produce protein that can be included in food in the long term.

As a result, the company Bioenergi Østjylland A/S was established in 2022 and subsequently merged into the newly established company ADA Green Energy, a joint company esta- blished by Danish Agro and Aura Energi to invest in green renewable energy projects.

Through joint investments, the new company ADA Green Energy will help secure the future supply of renewable energy in Denmark and make significant progress in the green transition.

The aim of the new company is to produce at least 200 GWh annually by 2025. This is equivalent to the energy consumption of between 40-50,000 house- holds. The electricity produced will help to ensure that Danish Agro group’s electricity supply in Den- mark comes exclusively from renewable energy sour- ces in the long term.

Back in the spring of 2019, Danish Agro’s new biofuel plant in Skamby on north Funen became operational. Among other things, this biofuel plant uses residual products from Danish Agro’s grain production and the steam from the plant furnaces is conveyed via pipes to Danish Agro’s feed factory which is located right next to the biofuel plant. The steam is used to produce feed at the factory, resulting in less need for natural gas. In 2022 we again achieved a reduction in gas consumption at the Skamby plant compared to before the biofuel plant was put into service. In 2022 this resulted in a total saving of 731 tonnes of CO 2.

Danish Agro’s strategic focus on plant-based foods led in 2021 to the group investing in the hemp seed company Møllerup Brands. Hemp seeds are an exciting raw material with good nutritional and pro- duction properties, and, at the same time, hemp is a frugal plant to cultivate.

Furthermore, hemp seeds have good protein pro- perties, which will be tested as part of the group’s strategy to develop more locally produced protein. Through Møllerup Brands, Danish Agro acquired the spice company Karlsens A/S in 2022, thus strengthe- ning its focus on plant-based foods.

The acquisition of Karlsens A/S creates entirely new opportunities to expand the range of Danish, sustai- nable and plant-based foods.

Following this, in 2022 the company DAVA Foods Ingredients was established, which is a merger of the companies Karlsens A/S and Møllerup Brands. DAVA Foods Ingredients is a new plant-based business area under the Danish Agro group subsidiary DAVA Foods, which will strengthen the distribution and further processing of crops grown by Danish farmers.

In addition, Danish Agro invested in 2022 in the plant-based and organic food company Organic Plant Protein A/S, which produces plant-based meat substitute products based primarily on peas and horse beans. The acquisition is part of Danish Agro’s strategic focus on plant-based foods, and with the investment will go even deeper into the plant-based food market.

In 2022, Danish Agro also entered into a cooperation agreement with Agreena, a company that facilitates climate certificate trading. The cooperation agre- ement means that the two companies will jointly spread awareness of climate-friendly cultivation methods and the associated possibility of issuing cli- mate certificates among farmers. The climate certifi- cates are based on climate-friendly farming practices such as regenerative agriculture.

When a farmer switches to regenerative farming practices, greenhouse gas emissions are reduced and carbon is stored in the soil.

Agreena’s CO 2 e certification programme quantifies how much carbon is stored in the soil, and climate certificates are issued on this basis. In 2022, the focus has been on expanding the cooperation with Agre- ena to farmers in Denmark, Finland and the Baltic States, but the plan is to extend the cooperation to the entire Danish Agro group business area.

Food safety and quality At Danish Agro, there is a strong focus on quality and food safety, and it is important for the company to provide good healthy products to our customers. Danish Agro wants to ensure a high quality throug- hout the entire value chain, from farm to table, via a certified quality management system that focuses on resource management, the production of safe products, validation, verification and ongoing impro- vements.
